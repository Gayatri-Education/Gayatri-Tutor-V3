# What is a Fraction? {#frac-basics}

`level:1`
Imagine you have a delicious, fresh round pizza and you slice it into 4 equal slices. If you eat 1 slice, you ate one out of four parts. We write this as 1/4! A fraction simply tells us about equal parts of a whole.

`level:2`
Formally, a fraction is written as a/b. The top number (a) is called the **numerator**, which counts how many parts we are considering. The bottom number (b) is called the **denominator**, which tells us the total number of equal parts into which the whole is divided. Notice that the denominator can never be zero!

`level:4`
Fractions represent rational numbers and can also be understood as division: 3/4 is mathematically identical to 3 ÷ 4. Fractions where the numerator is smaller than the denominator are called proper fractions (< 1), whereas fractions where the numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator are improper fractions (≥ 1), which can be represented as mixed numbers.


# Equivalent Fractions {#frac-equivalent}

`level:1`
If you cut a chocolate bar into 2 equal halves and eat 1 half (1/2), or if you cut the exact same bar into 4 equal pieces and eat 2 pieces (2/4), you still ate the exact same amount of chocolate! That is what equivalent means: different numbers, but identical value.

`level:3`
Two fractions are called **equivalent** if they represent the exact same portion of the whole. You can find an equivalent fraction by multiplying or dividing BOTH the numerator and denominator by the exact same non-zero whole number. For instance: (1 × 3) / (2 × 3) = 3/6.

`level:5`
To test whether two fractions a/b and c/d are equivalent without dividing, you can use the cross-multiplication rule: a × d must equal b × c. If and only if the cross-products are equal, the fractions are equivalent. Simplifying a fraction to its simplest form involves dividing both terms by their Greatest Common Divisor (GCD).

```quiz
id: frac-equiv-q1
type: mcq
difficulty: 1
question: "Which fraction is equivalent to 1/2?"
options:
  - "2/3"
  - "2/4"
  - "3/5"
  - "1/4"
answer: 1
explanation: "Multiplying both numerator and denominator of 1/2 by 2 gives 2/4."
```

```quiz
id: frac-equiv-q2
type: mcq
difficulty: 3
question: "What is 12/18 in its simplest form?"
options:
  - "6/9"
  - "3/4"
  - "2/3"
  - "4/6"

explanation: "Dividing both 12 and 18 by their greatest common divisor (6) gives 2/3."
```


# Adding Like Fractions {#frac-operations}

`level:1`
When two fractions have the same bottom number (like denominators), adding them is as simple as counting! If you have 1 slice of an 8-slice pizza and get 2 more slices, you now have 1 + 2 = 3 slices out of 8, which is 3/8.

`level:2`
To add fractions with the same denominator, add the numerators together and keep the denominator unchanged: a/c + b/c = (a + b)/c. Never add the bottom numbers!

`level:4`
When adding like fractions results in an improper fraction, simplify it to a mixed fraction. For example, 4/5 + 3/5 = 7/5 = 1 and 2/5. Always verify if the resulting sum can be further reduced to lowest terms.

```quiz
id: frac-ops-q1
type: mcq
difficulty: 2
question: "What is 2/7 + 3/7?"
options:
  - "5/14"
  - "5/7"
  - "6/7"
  - "1/7"
answer: 1
explanation: "Since the denominators are the same, add the numerators (2 + 3 = 5) and keep the denominator (7), resulting in 5/7."
```

```quiz
id: frac-ops-q2
type: mcq
difficulty: 4
question: "What is 5/8 + 7/8 expressed as a mixed fraction in simplest form?"
options:
  - "12/8"
  - "1 and 4/8"
  - "1 and 1/2"
  - "2 and 1/4"
answer: 2
explanation: "5/8 + 7/8 = 12/8. Reducing 12/8 gives 3/2, which equals 1 and 1/2 as a mixed fraction."
```
