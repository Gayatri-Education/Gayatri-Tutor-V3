# Discovery and the Cell Theory {#cell-discovery}

`level:1`
Just as a large brick building is made of tiny individual bricks, all living organisms (plants, animals, and humans) are composed of tiny building blocks called **cells**. You cannot see most cells with the naked eye because they are microscopic!

`level:2`
Cells were first observed in 1665 by Robert Hooke when examining a thin slice of cork under a primitive microscope. He noticed honeycomb-like compartments and called them 'cells'. Later, scientists developed the Cell Theory: all living organisms are composed of one or more cells, the cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life, and all cells arise from pre-existing cells.

`level:4`
Organisms consisting of a single cell are termed **unicellular** (e.g., *Amoeba*, *Paramecium*), carrying out all vital life processes within that single unit. Multicellular organisms exhibit a hierarchy: cells organize into tissues, tissues into organs, organs into organ systems, and organ systems into the complete living organism.

# Parts of a Cell {#cell-structure}

`level:1`
A cell has three main parts: the outer protective skin called the **cell membrane**, a jelly-like fluid inside called **cytoplasm**, and a command center called the **nucleus** which acts like the brain of the cell.

`level:3`
Inside the cytoplasm float specialized structures called **organelles**:
- **Nucleus**: Contains genetic material (DNA in chromosomes) and directs all cellular activities.
- **Mitochondria**: Known as the 'powerhouse of the cell' because they generate cellular energy (ATP) through respiration.
- **Vacuoles**: Storage bubbles for water, nutrients, and waste products.

`level:5`
The nuclear envelope is double-membraned with nuclear pores that regulate RNA and protein traffic. Cells lacking a defined nuclear membrane are termed **prokaryotic** (e.g., bacteria), whereas cells possessing a membrane-bound nucleus and specialized membrane-bound organelles are **eukaryotic**.

# Plant vs Animal Cells {#plant-vs-animal}

`level:1`
Plants cannot run away from strong wind, rain, or heat. Therefore, plant cells have an extra tough outer wall called a **cell wall** that gives them rigidity and strength. Plant cells also have green kitchens called **chloroplasts** to make food from sunlight.

`level:3`
Major distinctions between plant and animal cells:
1. **Cell Wall**: Present in plant cells (made of cellulose) outside the cell membrane; absent in animal cells.
2. **Chloroplasts / Plastids**: Present in green plant cells containing chlorophyll for photosynthesis; absent in animal cells.
3. **Vacuoles**: Plant cells typically possess one large central permanent vacuole providing turgor pressure; animal cells have small, temporary vacuoles.
